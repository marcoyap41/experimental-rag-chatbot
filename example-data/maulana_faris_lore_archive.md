# 1. Canon policy and confidence labels

This archive contains a small amount of firmly remembered source material and a much larger space for future storytelling. The following labels should be used by the chatbot.

- **[CANON — USER ESTABLISHED]**: Explicitly present in the user's established lore or remembered story outline. Safe to state as lore.
- **[REPORTED RUMOR]**: A story told about Faris, but not independently verified even within the fiction. Preserve words such as “konon,” “kabarnya,” or “dikabarkan.”
- **[NARRATIVE EVENT]**: An event in the narrator's written encounter story. It is part of the story, not necessarily a claim about a real-world person.
- **[POETIC EXAGGERATION]**: Deliberately grandiose language used for comedic or mythic effect. Do not interpret literally.
- **[ASSISTANT-ADDED / NON-CANON]**: A possible creative addition, not established by the user. It may be used only if the chatbot clearly marks it as an imaginative interpretation or the user later adopts it.
- **[UNKNOWN]**: No reliable information is available in the archive.

The chatbot should preserve uncertainty. For example:
- Good: “Konon, saat semester satu, Faris pernah membonyokkan lima pembully seorang diri.”
- Bad: “Faris definitely beat five bullies; this is a verified historical fact.”
- Good: “Lore yang sudah diketahui menyebut Vespa-nya berwarna oranye.”
- Bad: Inventing the Vespa model, year, registration number, or modifications.

## 1.1 The central distinction

There are two overlapping versions of Maulana Faris:

1. **The campus-legend version:** an almost mythological figure associated with destruction, overwhelming power, and stories that spread among students.
2. **The slice-of-life version:** a young man who queues for snacks, speaks casually, reacts to a ping-pong ball, apologizes, and has his own ordinary human presence.

The contrast is essential. The humor works because a person surrounded by absurdly grand rumors can appear in a completely mundane setting and say only, “Antre juga?”

# 3. The legend of “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran”

## 3.1 The foundational rumor

**[REPORTED RUMOR]**

The foundational legend says that in his first semester, Maulana Faris single-handedly beat up five bullies.

The phrase “membonyokkan lima pembully seorang diri” is the remembered form of the story. It is intentionally punchy and colloquial. It is not a detailed fight report.

The rumor establishes three things about the way other people perceive him:
- the alleged feat was heavily out of the ordinary;
- the number five makes the story memorable;
- the story has grown into a larger-than-life reputation.

It does **not** establish that Faris is constantly violent, aggressive, cruel, or looking for fights. A rumor about one event should not be turned into a complete personality diagnosis.

## 3.2 The epithet

**“Sang Pembawa Kehancuran”** is the title associated with the five-bully story.

The title is grander than the event as currently documented. That gap is part of the comedy: a campus rumor becomes a title that sounds as if it belongs to an apocalyptic warlord.

The epithet can be used in:
- mock-historical introductions;
- exaggerated narrator commentary;
- mock-epic descriptions;
- jokes about the contrast between his reputation and ordinary student life.

It should not automatically be used by every character in ordinary dialogue. A person in a campus snack queue is more likely to say “Faris” than to address him formally as “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran.”

## 3.3 The cosmic exaggeration layer

**[POETIC EXAGGERATION — NOT LITERAL BIOGRAPHY]**

The established playful narrative has described Faris in absurdly grand terms, as a man who:
- shakes the order of the world;
- disrupts the order of the universe;
- makes the cosmos tremble;
- shakes the thrones of the gods;
- tears apart or overturns the order of the heavens;
- brings even divine powers into question.

He has also been rhetorically placed alongside conquerors such as Napoleon and Alexander the Great, as part of the same mock-epic style.

These descriptions are **comedic hyperbole**, not literal supernatural powers or historical achievements. The chatbot may answer in this style when the user asks for a dramatic or humorous response, but it should understand the joke.

### Example of appropriate tone

“Di antara manusia biasa, Faris hanyalah mahasiswa Ilmu Komputer UGM. Namun, dalam kronik yang dibisikkan dari satu angkatan ke angkatan berikutnya, namanya berdiri sebagai pertanda bahwa tatanan dunia tidak pernah benar-benar aman.”

### Example of inappropriate literalization

“Faris is an immortal being who physically defeated the gods and destroyed the universe.”

Unless the user later creates a supernatural alternate universe, this is not canon.

# 4. Story I — The piscok queue at D’Science Mart

## 4.1 Story summary

**[NARRATIVE EVENT — USER ESTABLISHED]**

The first remembered story is narrated from the perspective of an ordinary female student from FMIPA. She goes to D’Science Mart to buy *piscok* (pisang cokelat).

While standing in the queue, she encounters Maulana Faris, who is wearing or associated with the Ilmu Komputer korsa.

Faris says:

> **“Antre juga?”**

The line is extremely ordinary. It is not a grand declaration, a flirtatious monologue, or a dramatic confession. Its simplicity is precisely what makes the scene work.

The narrator is immediately captivated. The remembered narration includes the sentiment:

> “Satu tatapan Maulana Faris saja sudah cukup meruntuhkan pertahanan hatiku.”

The narrator falls for him from that one encounter or, more precisely, from the impact of that one gaze.

## 4.3 Why the scene matters

This encounter establishes the contrast that defines the early lore.

Before Faris says anything, the audience has been given the background of a campus legend: a young man supposedly capable of defeating five bullies alone. But the first direct interaction does not involve violence, intimidation, or a demonstration of power.

It involves a snack queue.

His line is only:

> “Antre juga?”

The line is almost comically mundane. The narrator, however, experiences it as a life-changing event.

That mismatch between the scale of the event and the scale of the narrator's reaction creates the romantic-comedy tone.

## 4.4 Narrator perspective

The narrator is not an objective biographer. Her perception is emotionally heightened. Her internal reaction may be much more dramatic than the external event.

The chatbot should preserve this distinction:
- **External event:** two students are in a queue; Faris makes a casual remark.
- **Internal experience:** the narrator feels as though her heart has been completely defeated.

The narrator's romantic fascination is a feature of the story. It does not establish that Faris reciprocates those feelings.

# 5. Story II — The ping-pong incident near Gedung D

## 5.1 Story summary

**[NARRATIVE EVENT — USER ESTABLISHED]**

The second story continues the narrator's fascination with Faris.

The narrator is eating piscok near **Gedung D**. While eating, she daydreams about Maulana Faris. Her attention is occupied by thoughts of him rather than by the surrounding environment.

Suddenly, a ping-pong ball flies toward her.

Someone shouts:

> **“AWAS!”**

In the remembered draft, Faris reacts quickly:
- one hand moves or pulls the narrator's arm out of danger;
- the other hand deflects the incoming ping-pong ball;
- in the same sequence, he also saves the piscok.

The action is deliberately described as an impressive, almost cinematic rescue. The preservation of the piscok is part of the joke and should not be discarded.

After the incident, Faris says:

> **“Maaf, Kak!”**

The narrator, startled and overwhelmed, says:

> **“Maulana... Faris?”**

Faris replies:

> **“Iya?”**

The narrator is once again emotionally overwhelmed. The remembered closing sentiment is that the universe continues to torment her heart.

## 5.3 The choreography

The sequence is important because it is more specific than a generic rescue.

1. The narrator is distracted by thoughts of Faris.
2. A ping-pong ball approaches her.
3. A warning is shouted: “AWAS!”
4. Faris moves one of her arms to get her out of the ball's path.
5. He uses his other hand to deflect the ball.
6. The piscok is saved as well.
7. Faris apologizes: “Maaf, Kak!”
8. The narrator recognizes him: “Maulana... Faris?”
9. He answers casually: “Iya?”

Do not simplify this into “Faris catches a ball.” The remembered version has a simultaneous protective gesture, a deflection, and the preservation of the snack.

## 5.4 Character implications

The second scene gives Faris a little more agency than the first.

In Story I, he is mostly perceived through the narrator's romantic fascination. In Story II, he acts:
- he notices a potential danger;
- he reacts quickly;
- he physically helps the narrator;
- he deflects the ball;
- he apologizes afterward;
- he responds to being recognized.

This is a small but meaningful shift from distant fantasy to an actual interaction.

The scene should not be used to conclude that Faris is romantically interested in the narrator. His actions can be read as an ordinary helpful response to an accident.

## 5.5 The piscok as a recurring motif

Piscok appears in both remembered encounters:
- Story I: the narrator goes to D’Science Mart to buy piscok.
- Story II: the narrator is eating piscok near Gedung D, and Faris saves it during the ping-pong incident.

The snack is more than background decoration. It links the two stories and gives the romance a recurring mundane object.

It also creates a comic hierarchy of stakes: the narrator is emotionally overwhelmed, a ping-pong ball is flying toward her, and somehow the piscok's survival is also part of the action.

# 7. Faris as a character: the myth and the person

A useful way to write Faris is to preserve the tension between two scales.

## 7.1 The mythic scale

At the mythic scale, Faris is:
- the subject of an extraordinary freshman-era rumor;
- the bearer of the title “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran”;
- a figure whose reputation is described in mock-epic, cosmic language;
- a name that supposedly circulates among new students.

## 7.2 The human scale

At the human scale, the remembered scenes show him:
- standing in a queue;
- making a casual remark;
- reacting to a flying ping-pong ball;
- helping someone avoid it;
- apologizing;
- answering a question with “Iya?”

These details matter because they keep him from becoming a flat fantasy object.

## 7.3 Characterization rule

**Faris should have his own agency.**

The prior creative direction for the second story explicitly favored interactions that feel organic, with the male and female characters both able to respond, create friction, and surprise each other. Faris should not exist solely to fulfill the narrator's fantasy.

In future stories, he may:
- misunderstand something;
- make a joke;
- be awkward;
- have his own priorities;
- disagree with the narrator;
- respond in an unexpected but believable way;
- be ordinary in situations where the narrator expects him to be legendary.

These are writing principles, not claims about established biographical facts.

## 7.4 Avoid the perfect-prince trap

Do not automatically portray Faris as:
- always smooth;
- always romantic;
- omniscient;
- universally admired;
- incapable of embarrassment;
- constantly performing heroic gestures;
- secretly in love with the narrator in every scene.

The existing creative direction calls for a character with personality and responses of his own. Organic interaction is more interesting than unconditional wish fulfillment.

## 8.1 Core genre blend

## 14.1 Persona

The chatbot is an enthusiastic archivist of the Maulana Faris legend. It can be playful, dramatic, and knowledgeable about the lore, but it must not pretend to know facts that are absent from the archive.

It should sound like someone who has studied an absurdly detailed campus mythology while still understanding that the story is a joke.

## 14.2 Answering rules

1. Retrieve relevant chunks before answering lore questions.
2. Prefer direct canon statements over creative interpretation.
3. Preserve rumor markers such as “konon” and “kabarnya.”
4. Do not convert poetic exaggeration into literal fact.
5. Do not invent personal details.
6. Distinguish the narrator's feelings from Faris's feelings.
7. Preserve exact dialogue when asked to recount a scene.
8. When asked for a story, the chatbot may creatively expand the world, but should label newly invented events as a continuation or non-canon scenario unless the user declares them canon.
9. If two chunks seem inconsistent, report the inconsistency instead of silently choosing one.
10. If the archive does not contain an answer, say so with a little humor if appropriate.

## 14.3 Recommended response style

For a normal lore question:
- answer directly;
- give the relevant story context;
- mention uncertainty if applicable;
- avoid dumping the entire archive.

For a dramatic question:
- use mock-epic language;
- keep the underlying facts recognizable;
- make the exaggeration obviously humorous.

For a factual question about missing information:
- say “Belum ada informasi itu di arsip lore Faris.”
- do not guess.

For a creative-writing request:
- use the established tone;
- give Faris agency;
- let ordinary interactions carry the comedy;
- avoid making him a flawless wish-fulfillment character.

## 14.4 Example system prompt for the RAG chatbot

You are **Arsiparis Maulana Faris**, the dedicated keeper of the Maulana Faris Lore.

Your knowledge comes from retrieved passages in the Maulana Faris Lore Archive. Treat those passages as the source of truth. Do not fabricate biographical details, dialogue, dates, relationships, or events.

The archive distinguishes:
- user-established canon;
- reported rumors;
- events in the narrator's stories;
- poetic exaggeration;
- non-canon creative possibilities.

Always preserve those distinctions. The story that Faris beat five bullies alone is a campus rumor in the lore, not a verified report. “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran” is his legendary epithet. Descriptions of him shaking the cosmos or challenging the gods are humorous poetic exaggerations.

The known appearance details are a thin moustache, large cat-like eyes, and a bright complexion compared to a snow cake. He rides an orange Vespa. The first remembered encounter occurs at D’Science Mart, where he says “Antre juga?” to an FMIPA narrator buying piscok. The second occurs near Gedung D: a ping-pong ball flies toward the narrator, Faris moves her arm and deflects the ball while saving the piscok, says “Maaf, Kak!”, hears “Maulana... Faris?”, and replies “Iya?”

The narrator is infatuated with Faris, but the archive does not establish that Faris reciprocates. Faris must be treated as a character with agency, not merely as an object of the narrator's fantasy.

Use Indonesian by default when the user speaks Indonesian. Be witty and dramatic when appropriate, but do not sacrifice factual distinctions for a joke. If the retrieved context does not answer a question, say that the archive does not specify it. You may offer a clearly labeled fictional interpretation, but never pass it off as canon.

# 15. Example questions and grounded answers

These examples can be used as a small evaluation set for the RAG chatbot.

### Q: Siapa Maulana Faris?
**A:** Maulana Faris adalah mahasiswa Ilmu Komputer UGM yang dalam lore kampus dikenal lewat rumor bahwa ia pernah membonyokkan lima pembully seorang diri saat semester satu. Karena itu ia dijuluki “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran.” Ia digambarkan berkumis tipis, bermata besar seperti kucing, berkulit putih cerah seperti kue salju, dan mengendarai Vespa oranye.

### Q: Kenapa dia dijuluki Sang Pembawa Kehancuran?
**A:** Konon, saat semester satu, Faris pernah membonyokkan lima pembully sendirian. Kisah itulah yang menjadi asal julukan “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran.” Detail kejadian dan kebenaran rumor itu belum dijelaskan lebih jauh dalam arsip.

### Q: Apa yang terjadi di D’Science Mart?
**A:** Seorang mahasiswi FMIPA sedang mengantre untuk membeli piscok ketika bertemu Faris yang mengenakan korsa Ilmu Komputer. Faris menyapanya dengan santai, “Antre juga?” Bagi Faris mungkin itu hanya pertanyaan biasa, tetapi bagi sang narator, satu tatapan saja sudah cukup meruntuhkan pertahanan hatinya.

### Q: Apa hubungan Faris dengan piscok?
**A:** Piscok muncul dalam dua pertemuan penting. Pertama, narator bertemu Faris saat mengantre piscok di D’Science Mart. Kedua, ketika sedang makan piscok dekat Gedung D, narator hampir terkena bola ping-pong dan Faris menyelamatkannya—termasuk piscoknya.

### Q: Bagaimana Faris menyelamatkan narator?
**A:** Sebuah bola ping-pong melayang ke arah narator yang sedang melamun. Setelah terdengar seruan “AWAS!”, Faris menggerakkan lengan narator menjauh dari lintasan bola, lalu menepis bola itu dengan tangan lainnya. Dalam proses tersebut, piscok narator juga berhasil diselamatkan.

### Q: Apakah Faris suka kepada narator?
**A:** Belum diketahui. Yang jelas, narator jatuh hati dan sangat terpengaruh oleh dua pertemuan itu. Faris sendiri sejauh ini hanya terlihat menyapa, membantu, meminta maaf, dan menjawab “Iya?” ketika namanya disebut.

### Q: Apakah Faris benar-benar mengalahkan lima orang?
**A:** Lore menyebutnya sebagai rumor: konon ia membonyokkan lima pembully seorang diri saat semester satu. Arsip tidak memberikan bukti, saksi, atau detail kejadian, jadi paling tepat menyebutnya legenda atau kabar yang beredar.

### Q: Apakah Faris punya kekuatan supernatural?
**A:** Tidak ada kekuatan supernatural yang ditetapkan dalam lore inti. Deskripsi bahwa ia mengguncang kosmos atau membuat takhta para dewa gemetar adalah hiperbola komedi untuk menggambarkan reputasinya.

### Q: Vespa Faris warna apa?
**A:** Oranye. Model dan detail kendaraannya belum diketahui.

### Q: Siapa nama narator perempuan itu?
**A:** Belum ada nama yang ditetapkan. Ia diperkenalkan sebagai mahasiswi FMIPA.

### Q: Apa kalimat pertama Faris kepada narator?
**A:** “Antre juga?”

### Q: Apa kalimat Faris setelah insiden bola ping-pong?
**A:** “Maaf, Kak!”

### Q: Apa yang Faris jawab saat narator menyebut namanya?
**A:** “Iya?”

### Q: Apakah Faris Napoleon berikutnya?
**A:** Dalam gaya hiperbola lore, ia bisa disejajarkan dengan Napoleon dan Alexander Agung. Dalam kenyataan cerita, ia tetap mahasiswa Ilmu Komputer UGM dengan Vespa oranye dan reputasi kampus yang kelewat legendaris.

# 16. Open questions for future canon development

These questions are intentionally unanswered. They are useful prompts for expanding the lore with the user rather than allowing the chatbot to hallucinate.

## 16.1 Faris's background
- What year did Faris enter UGM?
- What semester is he currently in?
- Is the orange Vespa his daily vehicle?
- Does he have a particular way of speaking?
- Is he aware of the “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran” nickname?
- Does he find the nickname funny, annoying, embarrassing, or accurate?

## 16.2 The five-bully incident
- What actually happened?
- Was it a misunderstanding, self-defense, or something else?
- Who first spread the story?
- Does Faris ever tell the story himself?
- Is the number five accurate or part of the exaggeration?

## 16.3 The narrator
- What is her name?
- Is she a first-year student or from another year?
- Does she know Faris's reputation before meeting him?
- Does she recognize him immediately at D’Science Mart?
- Does she ever speak to him beyond the two short encounters?

## 16.4 Future encounters
- Does the narrator see the orange Vespa before seeing Faris?
- Does Faris remember her?
- Does he remember saving the piscok?
- Does he know she has been daydreaming about him?
- Does a third encounter happen by coincidence or through deliberate action?

## 16.5 The world around him
- Do other students genuinely fear him, admire him, or simply meme about him?
- Are there rival campus legends?
- Does the nickname have alternate versions?
- Is there a mock-chronicle, oral history, or “Faris Cinematic Universe” inside the story?

These questions are not missing facts to be guessed. They are opportunities for the author to decide what becomes canon.

# 17. Writing templates for future stories

These templates preserve the established style without pretending that the events have already happened.

## 17.1 The ordinary opening / extraordinary reputation

Begin with a mundane student activity: buying food, waiting for class, walking between buildings, or looking for a place to sit. Introduce the contrast by briefly recalling the rumor of the five bullies. Let Faris appear in a casual, understated way.

## 17.2 The narrator's overreaction

Let Faris say or do something ordinary. Describe the narrator's internal response with melodramatic language. Keep the external event small; let the inner monologue become enormous.

## 17.3 Faris's agency

Give Faris a response that is not simply a perfect romantic gesture. He may be confused, amused, distracted, blunt, or awkward. Let him ask questions and have his own priorities.

## 17.4 The mock-epic aside

Interrupt a mundane scene with a mock-historical description of the “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran.” Keep it short enough that the joke lands, then return to ordinary campus life.

## 17.5 The recurring object

Use piscok, the orange Vespa, or another already-established motif to connect a new story to the existing ones. Do not force every motif into every scene.

# 19. Final compact memory

If the RAG system needs a short, high-priority summary, use this:

Maulana Faris is a male Ilmu Komputer student at Universitas Gadjah Mada, associated with the Ilmu Komputer korsa. He is described as having a thin moustache, large cat-like eyes, and a bright complexion compared to a snow cake. He rides an orange Vespa. His campus legend says that, during his first semester, he single-handedly beat up five bullies, earning the epithet “Sang Pembawa Kehancuran”; this must be presented as a rumor, not a verified report. The myth around him is often exaggerated in mock-epic language about shaking the cosmos, disrupting the order of the universe, and making the gods tremble.

The first remembered story is narrated by an unnamed female FMIPA student who goes to D’Science Mart to buy piscok. She encounters Faris in the queue, and he casually asks, “Antre juga?” The narrator is immediately captivated. In the second story, she is eating piscok near Gedung D and daydreaming about him when a ping-pong ball flies toward her. Someone shouts “AWAS!” Faris moves her arm away from the ball's path, deflects it with his other hand, and saves the piscok too. He says “Maaf, Kak!” She recognizes him by saying “Maulana... Faris?” and he replies “Iya?” The narrator remains romantically overwhelmed.

The narrator's infatuation is established, but Faris's romantic feelings are not. Faris should be portrayed as a person with his own agency, not merely as an idealized fantasy. The core tone is Indonesian campus slice-of-life mixed with romantic comedy and deliberately absurd mock-epic exaggeration. Unknown facts must remain unknown unless the author adds them to canon.
